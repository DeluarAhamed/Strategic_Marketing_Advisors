A local SEO checklist for small service businesses

Not getting enough local inquiries? Check your Google Business Profile, service pages, contact details and follow-up before spending more on marketing.

Strategic Marketing Advisors

October 25, 2026

When a competitor appears in local search and your business does not, it is tempting to buy another marketing tool or publish a page for every nearby town. Start with the basics instead. Customers need to know what you do, whether you serve them and how to contact you. Search engines need consistent, useful information about the same things.

This checklist is for owners of local and service businesses. Work through it in order and keep a record of what you change. SMA is based in Cashiers, North Carolina; the process also applies to businesses serving other local markets.

1. Check your actual business details

Write down your public business name, primary phone, website, hours and real service area. Compare them with your website and existing directory listings. Fix discrepancies that could send a customer to the wrong place or leave them calling outside your hours.

A PO box is a mailing address, not an office customers can visit. Do not publish a made-up office, use a town-centre map pin as your premises or create profiles for places where your business is not eligible. Check Google’s business representation guidelines before making profile changes.

2. Review your Google Business Profile

Confirm that you control the correct profile and that its category, services, website link and hours describe your actual operation. Read it as a first-time customer: can you understand what the business does and how to take the next step? Add relevant, current photos of your actual work where appropriate.

Google explains that local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. Completing your profile helps describe your business; it does not guarantee a position. See Google’s local ranking guidance.

3. Give each main service a useful page

A service page should answer a buyer’s questions: who it helps, which problem it addresses, what is included, where the service is available and how to ask about it. Use a descriptive heading and natural language. Do not repeat a city name in every sentence.

If you serve several places from one base, explain that service area honestly. Create a location page only when it contains useful, distinct information about how you serve that location. Replacing a town name in the same paragraph does not give customers a reason to choose you.

4. Make the mobile contact path work

Open your site on a phone. Find a service, read its main explanation and try the phone, email and inquiry routes. Confirm what happens after submitting a form. A button that looks successful but delivers nothing loses opportunities even if search visibility improves.

Put the next step in plain language. Explain whether the visitor is requesting a callback, opening an email or booking a confirmed appointment. See our our current signature services if your site makes this difficult.

5. Respond to feedback responsibly

Read your reviews for recurring concerns, not just the rating. Reply with a relevant acknowledgement and a practical next step. Keep personal or sensitive details out of public replies, especially when a complaint needs investigation.

Ask customers for honest feedback consistently. Avoid incentives for positive ratings and do not ask only satisfied customers while diverting everyone else. A sustainable process is more useful than a short burst of review requests.

6. Track inquiries and decide what to do next

Record your starting point: relevant search queries, website inquiries, phone inquiries and which ones fit your services. Ask how new customers found you. Review changes over a useful period instead of treating daily ranking fluctuations as business results.

If profile details are wrong, fix those first. If search visitors arrive but do not contact you, examine the page and offer. If inquiries arrive but do not become work, review qualification and follow-up. Each problem needs a different response.
